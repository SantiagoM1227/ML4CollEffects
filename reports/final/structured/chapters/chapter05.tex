\subsubsection{Interpretation}
\label{subsubsec:fno_interpretation}

The FNO results support the formulation of longitudinal collective effects as operator-learning problems. This interpretation is consistent with the structure of the wake convolution and the spectral representation of impedance. Nevertheless, the FNO also presents limitations. Its formulation is particularly natural on regular grids and for reduced one-dimensional or low-dimensional functional data. Extending the same direct-grid strategy to a complete six-dimensional phase-space density would be computationally prohibitive. The full beam-distribution problem is therefore addressed through a reduced latent representation.

\subsubsection{Latent-space diagnostics}
\label{subsubsec:latent_space_diagnostics}

A well-regularized latent space should not simply memorize the training samples. To investigate this point, the latent coordinates are compared with physically interpretable observables. In the present work, Pearson correlations are evaluated between the latent variables and beam statistics including coordinate means and RMS extensions, such as
\begin{equation}
\sigma_x,
\quad
\sigma_y,
\quad
\sigma_{\zeta},
\quad
\sigma_{p_x},
\quad
\sigma_{p_y},
\quad
\sigma_{\delta},
\end{equation}
together with
\begin{equation}
\langle x\rangle,
\quad
\langle p_x\rangle,
\quad
\langle y\rangle,
\quad
\langle p_y\rangle,
\quad
\langle\zeta\rangle,
\quad
\langle\delta\rangle.
\end{equation}

For a physical observable \(q\) and latent coordinate \(z_k\), the Pearson correlation coefficient is
\begin{equation}
r_{q,z_k}
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}(q,z_k)
}{
\sigma_q \sigma_{z_k}
}.
\label{eq:pearson_latent_physics}
\end{equation}

For each physical observable, the twenty latent coordinates with the largest absolute correlations can be displayed,
\begin{equation}
\left\{
z_{k_1},
\ldots,
z_{k_{20}}
\right\}
=
\operatorname{TopK}_{20}
\left(
|r_{q,z_k}|
\right).
\label{eq:top20_latent_correlations}
\end{equation}

\placeholderfigure
{Latent correlation diagnostics}

The overall distribution should be discussed relative to its observed concentration around zero.}

The global correlation distribution provides a complementary diagnostic. If most coefficients are concentrated around
\begin{equation}
r_{q,z_k}\approx 0,
\end{equation}
while only a sparse subset reaches moderate absolute values, then the latent representation does not exhibit a simple one-to-one encoding of physical observables. In the present results, the correlations are sparse, the global distribution is centered around zero, and only a limited number of latent coordinates exhibit appreciable correlations with individual physical statistics. Moreover, the observed absolute correlations remain below approximately
\begin{equation}
|r_{q,z_k}|<0.5
\end{equation}
for the dominant cases.

This result is compatible with the intended role of the CVAE latent representation. The latent coordinates are not required to coincide individually with specific physical observables. Rather, they are expected to provide a distributed reduced representation of the projected density structure. The fact that only a limited subset of latent coordinates carries moderate information about individual observables suggests sparse physical alignment rather than a trivially encoded hidden parameterization.

Complete disentanglement is neither expected nor necessarily desirable. The relevant question is whether the latent state preserves the dynamical degrees of freedom required for propagation while avoiding excessive leakage of scale and position information that can be handled explicitly through physical conditioning variables or the auxiliary physical-dimensions head.

\subsection{Transformer latent propagation and the effect of locality}
\label{subsec:results_transformer_locality}
% =============================================================================

\subsubsection{Global-attention baseline and reconstruction artifacts}
\label{subsubsec:results_nowindow_transformer}

The first sequence-model baseline investigates whether an expressive Transformer can propagate the CVAE latent representation through the ordered lattice description. In this formulation, the causal mask prevents future or downstream tokens from influencing earlier positions, thereby enforcing upstream-to-downstream information flow. A global causal mask does not, however, impose finite interaction support: the state associated with a given position may, in principle, aggregate information from every preceding token.

This distinction is relevant because the beam state results from an ordered composition of lattice transformations. Although long-range dependence may be physically meaningful, unrestricted upstream mixing can introduce an architectural bias that differs from a predominantly local composition of element-wise transport. The no-window Transformer diagnostics provide a qualitative illustration of this effect.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]
{images/results/png/NOWINDOW_marginals_TF.png}
\caption{
Representative marginal reconstructions obtained with the no-window Transformer baseline. The figure is interpreted as a diagnostic of decoded distributional structure rather than solely as a pixel-wise reconstruction test. Particular attention is paid to coherent deformation of the high-density core, smoothing of localized structures, and systematic displacement relative to the reference marginals.
}
\label{fig:tf_nowindow_marginals}
\end{figure}

The observed discrepancy is more informative when evaluated through conditional statistics. For a two-dimensional marginal density
\begin{equation}
H(u,v),
\end{equation}
the conditional mean of \(v\) at fixed \(u\) is
\begin{equation}
m_{v\mid u}(u)
=
\frac{
\int v\,H(u,v)\,\dd v
}{
\int H(u,v)\,\dd v
},
\label{eq:conditional_mean_v_given_u}
\end{equation}
with an analogous definition for \(m_{u\mid v}(v)\). These quantities probe local organization inside a marginal and can therefore expose errors that remain partially hidden by global histogram metrics.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]
{images/results/png/artifact_nowindow_conditionals.png}
\caption{
Conditional-statistics diagnostic for the no-window Transformer. The comparison probes agreement of local conditional means rather than only global moments. Structured departures from the ideal parity relation indicate that the predicted distribution may reproduce part of the global support while distorting internal phase-space dependence.
}
\label{fig:tf_nowindow_conditional_artifact}
\end{figure}

Let \(H_{ij}\) denote a reference two-dimensional marginal and
\(\widehat{H}_{ij}\) its prediction. The residual field is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{ij}(u,v)
=
\widehat{H}_{ij}(u,v)
-
H_{ij}(u,v).
\label{eq:histogram_residual_field}
\end{equation}
A systematic reconstruction artifact corresponds to a nonzero mean residual
\begin{equation}
b_{ij}(u,v)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\varepsilon_{ij}(u,v)
\right]
\neq 0.
\label{eq:residual_bias_revised}
\end{equation}

At the latent level, an accumulated error mechanism can be represented schematically as
\begin{equation}
z_{n+1}
=
z_n
+
\Delta z_n^{\mathrm{true}}
+
\eta_{\theta,n},
\end{equation}
which gives, after \(N\) propagation steps,
\begin{equation}
z_N
=
z_0
+
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
\Delta z_n^{\mathrm{true}}
+
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
\eta_{\theta,n}.
\label{eq:latent_error_accumulation}
\end{equation}
If the learned local error has a systematic component,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\eta_{\theta,n}
\right]
\neq 0,
\end{equation}
the decoded rollout can develop a coherent distortion rather than a zero-centered random error. This mechanism does not prove that unrestricted attention is uniquely responsible for the observed artifacts. It nevertheless motivates the controlled locality study introduced next.

\subsubsection{Local-window Transformer}
\label{subsubsec:results_window_transformer}

To test whether unrestricted upstream mixing contributes to the observed reconstruction error, the Transformer attention support is restricted through the local causal mask introduced in Eq.~\eqref{eq:window_mask_revised}. In the principal Transformer--GNO comparison presented below, the Transformer implementation uses an attention window
\begin{equation}
w=8.
\end{equation}
Thus, the hidden state at sequence position \(i\) can only aggregate information from a finite upstream neighborhood.

The effect of this restriction is evaluated first through the longitudinal marginal
\begin{equation}
(\zeta,\delta),
\end{equation}
corresponding to pair index
\begin{equation}
p=11.
\end{equation}
This marginal is especially informative because it simultaneously probes longitudinal position, energy deviation, and their local correlation structure.

\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]

The local-window result is important because the architectural modification does not change the underlying CVAE representation or the physical prediction target. Instead, it changes the support of the interaction mechanism used during latent propagation. The resulting comparison therefore isolates, to first order, the role of locality as an inductive bias.

\subsubsection{Interpretation of the Transformer--GNO comparison}
\label{subsubsec:tf_gno_interpretation}

The comparison suggests that model performance is controlled not only by generic expressivity but also by the support and structure of the learned interaction mechanism. The no-window Transformer permits dense upstream mixing. The local-window Transformer restricts this support. The GNO instead defines information transfer directly through graph connectivity and a learned kernel.

Schematically, local-window attention computes
\begin{equation}
y_i^{\mathrm{TF}}
=
\sum_{
j\in\mathcal{N}_{w}(i)
}
\alpha_{ij}V_j,
\label{eq:window_attention_result_view}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}_{w}(i)
=
\left\{
j
:
0
\le
i-j
\le
w
\right\},
\label{eq:window_neighborhood_results}
\end{equation}
while the GNO computes
\begin{equation}
y_i^{\mathrm{GNO}}
=
\sum_{
j\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{GNO}}(i)
}
K_{\theta}
\left(
h_i,
h_j,
v_i,
v_j,
\mu
\right)
v_j.
\label{eq:gno_result_view}
\end{equation}

The two architectures therefore impose locality differently:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{
M_{ij}^{(w)}
\operatorname{softmax}
\left(
\frac{
Q_iK_j^{\top}
}{
\sqrt{d_k}
}
\right)
}_{\text{masked attention interaction}}
\qquad
\longleftrightarrow
\qquad
\underbrace{
\mathbf{1}_{
\left\{
j\in\mathcal{N}(i)
\right\}
}
K_{\theta}(i,j)
}_{\text{graph-kernel interaction}}.
\label{eq:local_kernel_correspondence_results}
\end{equation}

The empirical tendency of the local-window Transformer to approach the GNO prediction is therefore consistent with a common structural interpretation: both models constrain the support of information transfer relative to dense global attention. This does not establish an exact equivalence between attention and graph operators. It does, however, motivate locality as a central inductive bias to be studied quantitatively.

\subsection{Window-size dependence}
\label{subsec:results_window_study}
% =============================================================================

To investigate the locality hypothesis directly, Transformer models are trained with
\begin{equation}
w
\in
{
2,
10,
100,
1000,
10000
}.
\label{eq:window_values_study}
\end{equation}
The smallest values enforce strongly local information transfer, while sufficiently large windows approach dense causal attention over the available sequence length.

For each window, the same validation protocol should be applied. Let
\begin{equation}
E_p(w)
=
\frac{
\norm{
\widehat{H}_{p}^{(w)}
-
H_p
}_2
}{
\norm{
H_p
}_2
+
\varepsilon
}
\label{eq:window_relative_error}
\end{equation}
denote the relative error for projection \(p\). A global window-dependent score may be defined as
\begin{equation}
\overline{E}(w)
=
\frac{1}{15}
\sum_{p=1}^{15}
E_p(w),
\label{eq:window_mean_error}
\end{equation}
with a worst-projection score
\begin{equation}
E_{\max}(w)
=
\max_{p}
E_p(w).
\label{eq:window_max_error}
\end{equation}

The window study provides a controlled test of the central interpretation developed above. If intermediate local windows consistently reduce reconstruction error or residual bias relative to effectively global attention, the result supports the hypothesis that unrestricted latent mixing is not optimal for the present beam-transport task. Conversely, degradation at excessively small \(w\) would indicate that physically relevant dependencies extend beyond the nearest elements.

\subsection{Physics-statistics prediction}
\label{subsec:results_physics_statistics}
% =============================================================================

Distributional reconstruction should be complemented by direct prediction of physically interpretable beam statistics. For the six-dimensional state,
\begin{equation}
\xi
=
(x,p_x,y,p_y,\zeta,\delta),
\end{equation}
the principal first- and second-order statistics are
\begin{equation}
m_d
=
\langle\xi_d\rangle,
\qquad
\sigma_d
=
\sqrt{
\left\langle
(\xi_d-m_d)^2
\right\rangle
}.
\label{eq:physics_statistics_definition}
\end{equation}

A dedicated statistics head can be evaluated through parity relations
\begin{equation}
\widehat{m}_d
\approx
m_d,
\qquad
\widehat{\sigma}_d
\approx
\sigma_d,
\label{eq:physics_statistics_parity}
\end{equation}
together with coordinate-wise biases
\begin{equation}
B_{m_d}
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\widehat{m}_d-m_d
\right],
\qquad
B_{\sigma_d}
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\widehat{\sigma}_d-\sigma_d
\right].
\label{eq:physics_statistics_bias}
\end{equation}

A corresponding mean absolute error may be defined as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAE}_{m_d}
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left|
\widehat{m}_d-m_d
\right|
\right],
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAE}_{\sigma_d}
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left|
\widehat{\sigma}_d-\sigma_d
\right|
\right].
\end{equation}

This validation is important because a model may reproduce normalized marginal shapes while misestimating their physical location or extension. The statistics predictor therefore probes information complementary to the histogram decoder and directly tests whether latent propagation preserves beam-scale observables.

\subsection{Key finding: locality as a controlling inductive bias}
\label{subsec:key_finding_locality}
% =============================================================================

Taken together, the no-window artifacts, the local-window reconstruction, the GNO diagnostics, and the direct pairwise comparisons motivate a common interpretation. The principal distinction between the propagators is not simply parameter count or generic expressive power; it is the structure assigned to information transfer along the lattice.

The no-window Transformer permits dense causal mixing. The windowed Transformer constrains the support of its attention interaction. The GNO imposes a graph-defined support and parameterizes the interaction through an explicit learned kernel. The observed convergence of the windowed Transformer predictions toward both the reference distributions and the GNO behavior is therefore consistent with locality acting as a useful inductive bias for the latent beam-dynamics problem.

The resulting scientific interpretation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{global causal mixing}
;\longrightarrow;
\text{localized attention}
;\longrightarrow;
\text{graph-supported kernel propagation}
}
\label{eq:locality_synthesis_results}
\end{equation}
rather than a simple ranking of unrelated machine-learning models.

This interpretation remains empirical and should not be overstated as proof that the underlying collective dynamics are strictly local. Wake-mediated interactions can be nonlocal, and the accelerator map accumulates information over extended propagation distances. The result instead suggests that the \emph{support and geometry of the learned effective interaction} are central design choices. The window-size study provides the natural quantitative test of this hypothesis, while the GNO offers an alternative architecture in which interaction support is built directly into the graph representation.

\subsubsection{Global-mixing artifacts}
The main limitation of the baseline Transformer is that causal masking does not imply locality. Each token can attend to all previous tokens. In a physical lattice, however, many interactions are local or approximately local along the sequence. This mismatch can lead to structured residuals in the decoded distributions.

Let $H_{ij}$ denote a true 2D marginal and $\widehat{H}_{ij}$ its prediction. The residual field is
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_{ij}(u,v)=\widehat{H}_{ij}(u,v)-H_{ij}(u,v).
\end{equation}
A systematic artifact corresponds to a nonzero residual bias
\begin{equation}
    b_{ij}(u,v)=\E[\varepsilon_{ij}(u,v)]\neq 0.
    \label{eq:residual_bias_revised}
\end{equation}
This bias can arise from accumulated latent errors. If the learned update is
\begin{equation}
    z_{i+1}=z_i+\Delta z_i^{\mathrm{true}}+\eta_{\theta,i},
\end{equation}
then after $N$ steps,
\begin{equation}
    z_N=z_0+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta z_i^{\mathrm{true}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{\theta,i}.
\end{equation}
If $\E[\eta_{\theta,i}]\neq 0$, the rollout develops a systematic drift that appears as biased projected marginals after decoding.

\subsection{Locality as the controlling inductive bias}
The comparison suggests that the central issue is not only model size or generic expressivity. The decisive factor is whether the architecture encodes an inductive bias compatible with beam transport. The baseline Transformer is expressive but globally mixing. Window attention restricts this mixing and improves the result. The GNO goes further by representing the update as a learned local kernel over a directed graph.

This connection can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\operatorname{softmax}\big(QK^{\top}\big)V}_{\text{attention kernel}}
    \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
    \underbrace{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}K_{\theta}(h_i,h_j)v_j}_{\text{graph operator kernel}}.
\end{equation}
The windowed Transformer and the GNO therefore approach the same physical principle from different architectural directions. The former imposes locality by masking a global attention operator; the latter builds locality directly into the graph-kernel representation. This observation is the main scientific synthesis of the thesis.
